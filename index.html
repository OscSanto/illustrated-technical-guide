Chapter 1

What problem are we solving?

Imagine a customer sends a short message. An automated service must decide which team should handle it, whether a refund is requested, and how urgent the request is. These are structured decisions: the answer space is known before the model runs.

Running example · support ticket“I was charged twice for my order. Please refund the duplicate today.”

Only drafting the reply requires unconstrained text generation. The other tasks can be expressed as classifications, scores, or binary answers.

Why it matters

Generating several tokens to express a short categorical answer can be unnecessary work. A decision-oriented interface aims to expose the answer distribution directly, while preserving meaningful uncertainty.

Chapter 2

From transformer to classifier

A transformer turns a sequence of token embeddings into contextual hidden states. A classification head converts a selected representation into one score per candidate label.

p(y | x) = softmax(W h(x) + b)

Here h(x) is a hidden representation of the input, W is a learned projection matrix, and b is a bias vector. Softmax normalizes the resulting logits into probabilities that sum to one.

A numerical example

Suppose the three logits are [2, 1, 0]. Exponentiating and dividing by the sum gives approximately [0.665, 0.245, 0.090]. The highest-scoring label is chosen, but the full distribution is retained for downstream policy.

Implementation checkpoint

Implement the softmax function from scratch and verify that its outputs sum to one. Then compare the result against PyTorch's torch.softmax.

Chapter 3

One state, several decisions

The next architectural change is to reuse a shared representation of the same input for several typed questions. The central design choice is how each question reads that representation.

h = fθ(x)   ·   pj = softmax(gj(h, qj))

In this conceptual formula, x is the observed state, q_j is the j-th question, and g_j scores its available answers. It is a teaching abstraction, not a claim about any proprietary architecture.

What changed?

Fixed classifierFast and compactLabels fixed during training
Textual candidate scoringFlexible labelsMay repeat context processing
Shared-state decision readoutMany questions per contextRequires careful calibration and isolation
Chapter 4

Build the first version

Begin with a pretrained encoder and a small supervised head. Train it on labeled examples, validate its probabilities, and measure latency before adding architectural complexity.

import torch

logits = torch.tensor([2.0, 1.0, 0.0])
probabilities = torch.softmax(logits, dim=-1)
print(probabilities)  # tensor([0.6652, 0.2447, 0.0900])

Training procedure

Tokenize labeled examples, compute hidden states, project to class logits, calculate cross-entropy, backpropagate, and update weights. Keep a held-out validation split for calibration and model selection.

Chapter 7

Calibration

A probability of 0.8 should correspond to roughly 80% empirical correctness across comparable predictions. Reliability diagrams and proper scoring rules help reveal overconfidence.

Chapter 8

Training for decisions

Define the label space, choose an objective, balance examples, evaluate out of distribution, and measure the impact of alternative prompts and answer ordering.
