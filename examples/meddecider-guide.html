CHAPTER 01

The decision problem

Clinical notes contain assertions, negations, dates, medications, allergies and missing facts. A decision pipeline needs a structured answer to a specific question, not an open-ended paragraph. MedDecider is TheLion.AI’s family of open-weight decision models, released in October 2026. Its published interface takes a state, a question and 2–10 options and returns a probability for each option.

Scope. This is an educational reconstruction of the published inference and training method, not a clinically validated workflow or medical device.

Running example

A fictional note reports a platelet count of 269 × 10⁹/L but gives no hemoglobin value. Asked whether hemoglobin is at least 12 g/dL, the appropriate response is “not enough information,” not “no.” The published MedDecider-31B example assigns that option 0.998 probability. [1]

CHAPTER 02

Why not generate an answer?

A chat classifier normally generates tokens, then an application parses the output. That introduces formatting failure and makes a model’s written “confidence” difficult to interpret. MedDecider instead reads the next-token logits for option letters without sampling a response.

The project reports an internal 4B H100 comparison on a 448-token note: 45 ms for letter-logit readout versus 506 ms for 16 generated tokens and 15.7 s with 512 reasoning tokens. These are project-specific measurements, not independently reproduced timings. [1]

CHAPTER 03

Architecture and prompt construction

The released models are LoRA adapters applied to instruction-tuned causal language models. They do not introduce a new classifier head or special answer token. The inference loader reads the base model from the configuration, loads the adapter and merges it into the model weights. [1][2]

State: Platelets 269 × 10^9/L; no hemoglobin recorded.
Question: Is hemoglobin at least 12.0 g/dL?
Options:
A. Meets the criterion
B. Does not meet the criterion
C. Not enough information in the note
Answer with the letter of the correct option only.

Each option is assigned a letter from A through J. The tokenizer matters: the implementation must select the intended token IDs for these letters under the base model’s tokenizer and prompt template.

Exact public interface and boundaries

MedDecider.decide(state, question, options, qtype="choice") accepts a text or JSON state (or None), a plain-language question, and between two and ten answer options. The three types—choice, noul, and score—select different calibration temperatures; they do not select different neural heads. The reference loader reads the base model name from meddecider_config.json, applies the PEFT LoRA adapter, merges it, and disables reasoning for the chat-template prompt. The next-token letter scores are extracted without sampling.

Important implementation caveat: tokenization of A–J must be checked for the particular chat template. The provided educational NumPy lab starts after the vocabulary-logit readout, so it does not claim to reproduce the original model weights or tokenizer behavior.

Source: MedDecider primary project page; numbers are developer-reported, not independently reproduced.

CHAPTER 04

Derive the probability readout

Let z be the final-position vocabulary logit vector. Select only the entries for the available option letters. For K options and temperature T, the conditional probability of option i is:

pᵢ = exp(zᵢ / T) / Σⱼ₌₁ᴷ exp(zⱼ / T)

The softmax is restricted to the letters that represent offered options. Scores for other vocabulary tokens do not enter the denominator. This makes the probabilities sum to one across the supplied alternatives.

Numerical walkthrough

Take illustrative logits [2, 1, 0] with T = 1. Exponentials are 7.389, 2.718 and 1.000; their sum is 11.107.

Numerical stability: subtract the largest logit before exponentiating. This leaves the probabilities unchanged and prevents overflow.

CHAPTER 05

Reverse the options to reduce position bias

Letter-based scoring is vulnerable to positional preferences. The published default performs two scoring passes: original order and reversed order. It maps both outputs back to the original semantic options before averaging. [1][2]

p̄ᵢ = ½ [ pᵢ(original) + pᵢ(reversed, remapped) ]

The project reports that reversing options changed the base Qwen3.5-4B answer on 45.3% of MedQA items, versus 9.5% for MedDecider-31B and 7.3% for MedDecider-27B. The second pass doubles the number of scoring forward passes; it does not eliminate bias. [1]

CHAPTER 06

Temperature scaling and calibration

Temperature controls sharpness. When T > 1, the distribution becomes less concentrated; when T < 1, it becomes sharper. Temperature is fitted on held-out labeled examples, not chosen from a single demonstration.

T* = argminₜ Σₙ −log softmax(z⁽ⁿ⁾ / T)ᵧₙ

For choice questions, the reported temperatures are 1.33 (4B), 1.43 (9B), 1.23 (27B) and 1.49 (31B). The published noul and score temperatures are 1.0. The project reports an expected calibration error of 0.029 for the 31B on 12 unseen datasets. That does not guarantee calibration on local clinical records. [1]

CHAPTER 07

Training: supervised letter logits

Training examples contain a state, question, options and correct option. The answer options are shuffled during training, making the correct semantic answer appear under different letters. The model is optimized with cross-entropy over only the offered option-letter logits.

L = −log p(y | state, question, options)

For the example distribution [0.665, 0.245, 0.090], if A is correct, L = −ln(0.665) ≈ 0.408. The derivative with respect to the selected logits is ∂L/∂zᵢ = pᵢ − 1[i=y] at T=1. For A, the gradient is approximately [−0.335, 0.245, 0.090]. Gradient descent therefore pushes the correct logit up relative to the alternatives.

The project reports LoRA applied to all linear layers, rank 32 for 4B/9B and rank 16 for 27B/31B. The released adapters are considerably smaller than the full base models. [1]

CHAPTER 08

Dataset design: minimal pairs

Clinical classification fails when models rely on a keyword without interpreting its context. Minimal pairs are two examples differing in one decisive fact. This creates a sharper training signal than simply collecting more vaguely similar notes.

Other important contrast sets include negated versus active diagnoses, family history versus patient history, missing versus explicit negative evidence, and discontinued versus current medication. The 4B training mix is reported as 119,034 decisions; larger variants use a different mix of 35,800 decisions. These are not identical training regimes. [1]

CHAPTER 09

A minimal reproducible implementation

The following CPU-only NumPy code reconstructs the probability readout and reverse-order aggregation. It does not run MedDecider weights. Supply real letter logits from a compatible causal language model to connect it to inference.

import numpy as np

def softmax_temperature(logits, temperature=1.0):
    z = np.asarray(logits, dtype=np.float64) / temperature
    z -= z.max()
    e = np.exp(z)
    return e / e.sum()

def average_orders(original_logits, reversed_logits, temperature=1.0):
    p1 = softmax_temperature(original_logits, temperature)
    p2 = softmax_temperature(reversed_logits, temperature)[::-1]
    return (p1 + p2) / 2

p = average_orders([2.0, 1.0, 0.0], [0.0, 1.0, 2.0])
assert np.isclose(p.sum(), 1.0)
assert np.all(p >= 0)
print(np.round(p, 3))  # [0.665 0.245 0.09 ]

Connect the readout to an actual model

Load the model and merged adapter using the published meddecider_infer.py script. Construct the exact chat-template prompt, run a forward pass, take logits[0, -1, :], select the option-letter token IDs, then apply the algorithm above. Do not substitute raw text generation and treat its output as calibrated confidence.

Clinical applications and action aggregation

Trial pre-screening: a fictional study uses three inclusion and two exclusion criteria. Each criterion is a separate three-option decision: meets, does not meet, or insufficient evidence. The tutorial uses a 0.8 threshold to invite for full screening, exclude, or send to a coordinator—not to enroll patients automatically.

Medication reconciliation: one drug can be described as both discontinued and an allergy. The source reports an amoxicillin example with probabilities 0.466 and 0.525 for those two mutually exclusive response options. Since both imply “not currently taking,” grouping those actions produces a reported aggregate probability of 0.994 after including the small on-hold probability. Summing is appropriate only for disjoint answer categories that map to the same action.

Evidence classification: trial statements can be supported, contradicted or absent from the source. Abstract screening is proposed, not benchmark-validated. The published workflows use synthetic records and have not been clinically validated.

Source: MedDecider primary project page; numbers are developer-reported, not independently reproduced.

CHAPTER 10

Routing decisions and abstention

Probabilities are useful only when linked to actions and a validated threshold. A missing-evidence option is an explicit candidate answer, not a magical hidden abstention mode.

In medication reconciliation, several options can map to the same downstream action. If “stopped,” “on hold” and “allergy” all imply “not taking,” their mutually exclusive probabilities can be summed. The original tutorial illustrates a combined probability of 0.994 for that group. Grouping independent sigmoid outputs would not have the same interpretation. [1]

CHAPTER 12

Robustness, safety and deployment

Untrusted notes can contain instructions aimed at the model. The correct behavior is to interpret those strings as clinical evidence only when relevant, not as instructions governing the classifier. The project’s planted-instruction evaluation reports 2.7% compliance for the 31B and 13.7% for Jev 1.13; neither is zero. [1]

Validation checklist

  • Use a de-identified, labeled local evaluation set.
  • Refit temperature on calibration data separate from test data.
  • Measure errors by subgroup, document type and source system.
  • Test negation, missing facts, medication status and prompt injection.
  • Record uncertainty and route ambiguous cases to qualified reviewers.
  • Respect noncommercial adapter licensing and base-model terms.
Clinical warning: MedDecider is a research model, not a medical device. None of the project’s example workflows is clinically validated. Do not use this educational page for patient-care decisions.

CHAPTER 13

Reproduction checklist and exercises

To recreate the published mechanism, begin with a working base model and the public inference script, not a new classifier head. Then test the decision readout independently before fine-tuning anything.

Exercises

  1. Recompute the softmax example at T = 1.5 and verify that the sum is one.
  2. Reverse a three-option prompt, remap its output and verify the averaged distribution.
  3. Implement negative log-likelihood and compare its numerical gradient to p − one_hot(y).
  4. Create 20 synthetic medication minimal pairs and record which ones a baseline keyword classifier misses.
  5. Fit a scalar temperature on held-out synthetic logits, then evaluate ECE on a separate split.

Reproduction checklist

  1. Choose the published base model and check its separate license.
  2. Load the correct PEFT adapter, merge it, and apply the matching chat template with reasoning disabled.
  3. Build the state/question/A–J option prompt and verify option-letter token IDs.
  4. Extract final-position letter logits, apply the question-type temperature, and softmax only over offered letters.
  5. Score reversed options, remap by semantic option, and average the two distributions.
  6. Validate normalization, reversal invariance on symmetric examples, ECE, order sensitivity, and latency.
  7. Recalibrate on local labeled data; evaluate abstention and harmful false positives before any operational use.

The included NumPy lab implements steps 4–5 without loading weights. Reproducing the reported fine-tuning requires access to compatible training data, model weights, adequate compute, and careful licensing.

CHAPTER 14

What the source establishes—and what it does not

Verified from the project page: LoRA-adapted causal language models; next-token letter-logit readout; two-order averaging; question-type temperatures; published training mixes and reported benchmark results.

Demonstrations created for this guide: numerical logits, reverse-order example, flow diagrams, interactive temperature widget and NumPy reconstruction. They explain the mechanism but are not outputs from actual MedDecider inference.

Not verified here: independent benchmark replication, training a full adapter, executing the Hugging Face model, clinical validation or performance on real patient records. Those require model downloads, suitable GPU resources and a separate evaluation protocol.

Licensing, clinical validation, and evidence limits

The adapter release is CC BY-NC 4.0, which is non-commercial and not an OSI open-source license. Base-model licenses apply separately. The models are research artifacts, not medical devices. The reported training notes and tutorial records are synthetic, and the project has not clinically validated these workflows on real patient data. Published exam results may also be affected by base-model pretraining exposure.

Every performance figure in this guide is attributed to the project and model cards; no weights were downloaded and no full benchmark was rerun. Our executable test only validates the mathematical reconstruction of letter scoring and aggregation.
